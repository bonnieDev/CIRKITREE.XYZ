Bonnie Caroline Remeika is a creative technologist and UX/UI designer in Ravenna, Ohio, with twenty-five years across brand systems, front-end development, motion, audio and full-stack engineering. She spent eight years stewarding global design systems at GlaxoSmithKline and seven years running The Detroit Studio before going independent as RMK Systems, where she builds and licenses interaction IP. Recent work includes AETHER_COMMAND, a two-year solo build pairing an interactive globe over roughly 14,000 ancient genome samples with a narrative engine, and Lil Swaps, a shipped sustainability app. Eight provisional patents filed on interaction systems.

AETHER_COMMAND

Full-stack platform · Next.js, Mapbox GL, deck.gl · sole builder, two years

Interactive globe and narrative engine built on the AADR dataset of roughly 14,000 ancient genome samples, cross-referenced against artifact and isotopic data. Chaptered narrative engine, proximity-based spatial audio, provenance-gated save system.

Four AI agent voices are defined as Zod schemas rather than orchestration code, so the platform stays model-agnostic. The schema constrains output shape, not content. One of them drives the interface live: moves the map, highlights regions and generates charts in response to spoken questions.

Bunny Anarchy

Brand, characters and voice · single-file HTML site with a lazy-loaded React Three Fiber game

Princess Pusce, a crowned skull bunny, and Reggie, an origami unicorn. Deadpan satirical voice throughout. Chérie Bomba fronted by Caroline Usagi, a fictional creative director whose name is a trilingual pun.

Bunny Anarcade is a catch-the-drop game built into the storefront. A voxel rabbit catches falling merch while Reggie cheers streaks and laughs at misses, driven by his own state machine that only hears named game events. The game loads only when someone presses play. On page load, Reggie leaps across the hero stickers and does a headspin on his horn.

Kinedic Drop

Brand motion · vector mark, CSS keyframes, SVG displacement · Next.js

A brand mark that arrives instead of appearing. One drop falls, shifting from copper to blue, and its impact ripples the whole page through an SVG displacement filter before the mark, wordmark and tagline rise out of it. Built in code and vectors, no video.
